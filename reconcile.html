Why the numbers differ

  • Paper versus border. A statutory rate counts every duty the law imposes. A realized rate counts what was assessed on the goods that actually entered: exemptions, USMCA carve-outs, exclusions and trade shifting away from the highest rates all lower it. The gap is information, not error.
  • Calculated duty versus cash. ETRX uses the duty fixed at entry. Cash receipts arrive later and are reduced by refunds; after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, a refund process exceeding $100B makes cash-based measures hard to read for years. Calculated duty is immune to it.
  • Baskets. ETRX steel (HS 72 and 73, all origins) printed 36.15% in July 2026; Penn Wharton groups steel with aluminum. ETRX publishes vehicles per origin (EU, Mexico, Canada); Penn Wharton for all origins. Same data, different cuts.
  • Revisions. Census revises trade data. ETRX never restates a first print and publishes revisions separately; research outputs generally republish the revised figure.
  • Coverage. ETRX uses imports for consumption and excludes HS chapters 98 and 99 and AD/CVD duties. Aggregates that include them differ by construction.
